# Script style guide

Reverse-engineered from a ~5.5-min TikTok explainer about a Tomahawk strike on an Iranian missile factory (sections and timings below are from that video). Copy the *techniques*, never the lines — write original narration every time.

## Pace and voice

- ~150 words per minute in English, ~140 in Spanish. One idea per sentence.
- Present tense throughout the story beats ("are screaming", "banks", "goes stupid"). Past tense only inside explainers about history.
- Short sentences at tension peaks, longer ones when explaining. Fragments are fine: "Twelve missiles. A dozen targets. Zero margin for a miss."
- Vivid verbs and plain words: "goes stupid", "spends itself against a ridgeline", "the whole landscape shouts back".
- No hedging filler ("it's worth noting", "interestingly"), no "in this video", no channel plugs in the narration.

## Act structure (long version, ~5 min; scale up by adding acts)

| Time | Beat | What it does | Shots |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Cold open | Clock time, count, weapon, speed, altitude, target. Why this target matters. "No American weapon has ever reached this deep." | MAP, CLOCK, BROLL |
| 0:20–0:35 | Stakes | Every obstacle between attacker and target. Three-fragment rhythm. | MAP, STAT |
| 0:35–1:05 | Journey | Concrete sensory detail: shadow on gravel, a truck driver who hears the engine two seconds after the flash. Formation changes with terrain. | BROLL |
| 1:05–1:40 | The other side | Introduce the defenders as competent people with a hard problem. | BROLL |
| 1:40–2:08 | Reveal + spec card #1 | "What's flying up that riverbed? The BGM-109 Tomahawk Block V." Weight, length, speed, warhead, range, then an analogy ("Chicago to Denver at treetop height"). | SPEC, BROLL |
| 2:08–3:10 | Physics idea #1 | The radar horizon: why a radar that sees a fighter at 93 miles sees a 50-ft missile at ~20. Analogy: car headlights on a hilly road at night. | DIAGRAM, MAP |
| 3:10–3:44 | Action peak | The intercept attempt, second by second. The miss. | BROLL (video gen) |
| 3:44–4:07 | Spec card #2 | The defender's weapon, same card format, plus a cost comparison. | SPEC, STAT |
| 4:07–4:52 | Physics idea #2 | Why it missed: seeker handoff + ground clutter. Analogy: "a missile built to find a jet in an empty sky went looking for a shadow in a room full of furniture." | DIAGRAM, BROLL |
| 4:52–end | Consequence | What the outcome means; the target itself, explained concretely (what each building does). Size analogy ("two-thirds of Manhattan"). End on a forward-looking beat. | MAP, BROLL |

For 10–15 min long versions, add acts rather than slowing down: a second defender layer, the launch platform (ship/submarine), the history of the weapon, the mission planning, the aftermath. Each new act still gets one spec card or one physics idea.

## Short version (1–3 min)

```
0:00  Hook: clock + weapon + target in one sentence (≤2 s to the first number on screen)
0:05  Stakes in three fragments
0:12  Reveal + spec card (the attacker)
0:30  One physics idea with one analogy
0:55  Action peak
1:15  Consequence line — end hard, no outro
```

At 3 min you can afford a second spec card for the defender.

## Analogy bank (make new ones; reuse sparingly)

- Distance → city pairs the audience knows (Chicago→Denver, Madrid→Barcelona for Spanish).
- Area → Manhattan, football fields, a city's downtown.
- Speed → "a 737 at cruise", "faster than a rifle bullet" (check it's true).
- Radar horizon → headlights on a hilly road; a lighthouse on a short tower.
- Ground clutter → shouting into a canyon vs. an empty field; a room full of furniture.
- Cost → "a mid-size house", "the price of a fighter jet's tires" — and give the actual dollar figure too.

## Spanish adaptation

- Adapt, don't translate. Re-pick analogies for Spanish-speaking audiences where it helps (city pairs, units: give km and km/h; miles only if the source uses them, with conversion).
- Keep system names in their official form (BGM-109 Tomahawk, Khordad 15) and say them the same way each time.
- Trim ~15% so the Spanish voiceover lands near the English timing; check by word count (~140 wpm).
